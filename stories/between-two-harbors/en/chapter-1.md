# The Bus at Dawn

I took the earliest bus out of town because I wanted the departure to feel decisive. If I waited until after breakfast, my mother would have found another bowl of rice to slide toward me, and another reason to stay. Morning was easier. Morning let me pretend this was all part of a plan.

![Between Two Harbors cover](/media/stories/between-two-harbors/cover.png)

The coast disappeared slowly behind the window. First the fish market, then the bend in the road, then the harbor cranes that always looked larger in memory than they did in real life. I watched the shoreline shrink until it became a sentence I could no longer read.

## What I Thought I Was Leaving

I thought I was leaving a small town.

What I was actually leaving was a set of habits: waking before the heat, knowing every shopkeeper by name, counting money twice before spending it, and measuring weather by the smell of the wind. I thought those things were proof that I was ready to go. I did not yet know they were also the reason I would eventually return.

> Distance does not always make things smaller. Sometimes it reveals their shape.

By noon I had convinced myself that I was becoming someone new. By evening I was already missing the sound of waves hitting the breakwater.
