My father was a man of very few words and very many callouses. He spoke in actions the way some men speak in sentences.

## The Work

Every morning before I was awake, he had already gone. The fishing boats left before sunrise. He came home in the middle of the afternoon smelling of salt and diesel, ate quietly, and napped before the evening's second work began — the nets to mend, the traps to check, the engine to patch.

I never saw him read a book. I never saw him sit still without purpose. Rest, for him, was preparation for more work.

## What He Taught Without Saying

He never told me to study hard. He never gave speeches about opportunity or the future. But he drove me to every exam on his motorbike, waited outside in the heat for hours, and asked nothing when we rode home except: *"Was it fair?"*

I didn't understand the question then. I do now. He was asking whether I had been given a fair chance — not whether I had won.

> It was the question of a man who knew the outcome wasn't entirely in his child's hands — but the preparation was.
